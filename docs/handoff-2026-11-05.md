# Handoff, 2026-11-05

**Read this first.** What exists, what was measured, what was not done, and the first thing to do.

## In one paragraph
`fitforgephotoai` is a working prototype that builds a synthetic dataset of garments draped on
bodies with exact ease and elasticity labels and renders, driving the body model (annyextension)
and the solver (fitforgesolver) in-process. It was smoke-tested end to end on CPU and the first 8 samples of a 24-sample
demo were built. The 288-sample pilot and the rest of the demo were lost to session restarts (issue `2026-11-05-rest`).
The spec of what the three FitForge repos would need, and the decisions Keith has to take
(including that this reverses ADR-0080 decision 4), is `docs/spec-synthetic-ease-elasticity-dataset.md`.
No other repo was changed.

## Facts measured here (4-core cloud box, no GPU)
- Draft 1.3 s; drape 230 s with the solver's production settings at a 2 cm mesh (80 s with the
  plain defaults, but then the shirt slides off the shoulders); QC and labels under 2 s; four
  renders 5 s. About 60 samples an hour on 4 cores.
- Every drape ended at the 400-step ceiling unconverged (issue `2026-11-05-conv`).
- The solver's flat-pattern chart misreads waist and hip on a drafted shirt (issue `2026-11-05-chrt`);
  its mesh-measured ease is poisoned by the sleeves, so the builder has its own torso-only measure
  (`photoai/measure.py`, using the body model's tailor rule).
- The first smoke body (bust 113 cm) poked through the 2 cm cloth: QC fail, recorded as such.
- Four workers side by side slow each drape from about 245 s to about 660 s: about 22 samples an
  hour on this box, not 60. Background (`nohup`) workers died at every session restart; only
  short foreground rounds (`--limit 1` or `2` per worker) completed.
- **Every demo sample failed the solver's QC** on the penetration gate (2.5 to 6 % of vertices
  inside the body against a 2 % limit) at the 2 cm mesh. QC-clean samples need the production
  1 cm mesh, four times the triangles (spec S5).

## The adversarial review (ran, read-only, 2026-11-05)
It set out to prove the ease labels wrong and did, on four counts, all in the worn ease:
1. The body's tailor calibration factor (bust x1.06, hips x1.07) was applied to the cloth's
   outline, so worn ease at bust and hips was 6 to 7 cm too high against the pattern ease.
   **Fixed**: no factor on either side; the body is measured by the same slice rule at the same
   level on the same mesh (`body_girth_contour_cm` in the record, beside the tailor's numbers).
2. The waist subtracted the body model's landmark waist (a fixed ring on the rest mesh) from a
   slice girth. **Fixed** by 1.
3. The slice rule's 0.20 m central band clips sections wider than 40 cm. **Open**: issue
   `2026-11-05-band`; the label reads low on very loose garments and the largest bodies.
4. On a posed body the band no longer excludes arms, so worn ease would be off by tens of cm.
   **Fixed** by refusing any pose but `rest` in the runner until spec S8 is done.
5. `ease_pattern_cm` wrote waist and hip numbers the solver's chart reader had clamped to the
   hem. **Fixed**: bust only; the full chart stays in the garment block.
6. A pattern with no torso-named panel fell back to every face, sleeves included. **Fixed**: no
   girth at all.
Units, panel indexing, the ladder, the designed ease and the empty-dict paths were checked clean.
**The 8 demo records on disk were built before these fixes** and carry the old worn ease (bust
and hips scaled, waist against the landmark). Rerun the demo (delete `outputs/demo_cpu`) to
relabel; the table above is from the old records and its bust worn ease is about 6 cm high.
